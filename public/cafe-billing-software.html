A cafe is a queue, not a floor

Restaurant software mostly assumes tables: a guest is seated, orders over time, and settles at the end. A cafe works the other way round. Payment comes first, the guest may not sit down at all, and the whole interaction should take under a minute.

Run a cafe on table-shaped software and every order costs extra taps that exist for a situation you do not have. At eight in the morning with nine people waiting, that is the difference between a queue that moves and one that does not.

The other difference is repetition. A cafe sells the same fifteen things all day to people who order the same thing every day. Speed comes from making the common case fast, not from covering every case.

How token billing at a cafe counter works

  1. Take the order at the counter. The menu opens on the items you actually sell, so a flat white and a samosa are two taps rather than a search.
  2. Settle immediately. Cash, UPI, card or online. Payment happens before preparation, which is what a cafe expects and a table-service flow does not.
  3. The guest gets a token number. Printed on the bill, and the same number the kitchen sees.
  4. The counter or kitchen prepares it. The order appears on the kitchen screen or prints, marked with the token rather than a table.
  5. Call the token when ready. The order is marked served and clears from the live board.

What a cafe needs that a restaurant does not

Payment before preparation. The bill closes at the start, so the day's takings are complete without chasing open tables at closing.

Token numbers instead of table numbers. Guests move around, sit outside, or wait standing. A token follows the person; a table number does not.

A short menu, fast. Categories put the morning items where the taps are, and half and full sizes are separate priced items rather than a modifier to hunt for.

What a cafe usually does not need is the table floor view. If you also have seating, it is there — but the token flow does not require it.

Arranging a menu for speed

The single biggest influence on billing speed is not the software. It is how the menu is arranged.

Cafes that bill fastest put their ten highest-volume items in the first category, so the morning rush is one tap deep. Sizes are set as separate priced items rather than asked as a follow-up question. Seasonal and rarely-ordered items go in a category further down, where they cost a tap but do not slow every other order. Rearranging takes ten minutes and is worth more than any feature on this page.

Who this suits, and who it does not

It suits you if guests pay at a counter and wait for a number, your menu is short and repetitive, and your busiest problem is queue length.

It suits you less if you run a full table-service restaurant where guests order over an hour and settle at the end. That works here too, but you should read the table ordering page instead, because the flow that matters to you is the other one.

Questions cafe owners ask about cafe billing

Can I run token service and table service together?

Yes. A cafe with a few seated tables can take counter orders by token and seated orders against a table, on the same system and the same menu. The kitchen sees both, marked with whichever identifier the order carries.

Do token numbers restart each day?

Yes. The daily counter resets with the business day, so numbers begin again each morning rather than climbing indefinitely. Bill numbers work the same way, running in an unbroken sequence within each day.

How fast is billing a regular's usual order?

The menu opens on your own categories, so a common order is a few taps and a payment mode. There is no customer lookup step and no account to find, because a cafe order does not need one. Most of the speed comes from a short menu arranged the way you sell.

What happens if the internet drops during the morning rush?

Counter billing continues on the installed desktop or Android app, including token and bill printing, and syncs when the connection returns. QR ordering stops during an outage because it runs on the guest's phone, but a cafe taking orders at the counter is not affected.
